01 / The short answer

Our geometry experiment already returns 3D.

The current model predicts how a supplied healthy mesh should move. The 2D-mask proposal describes a separate track. A 2D overlay is still useful for explaining a 3D result, but it should not replace the geometry experiment’s stated output.

Current synthetic experiment · clarified 23 September

The exact input and output

Input

  • Healthy blade CAD mesh.
  • Seven inspection images and their matching healthy reference images.
  • Supplied camera information and image-to-healthy-mesh correspondence.

Output

  • Predicted vertex movement: Δx, Δy, Δz.
  • Estimated deformed blade mesh.
  • Visualization of predicted surface changes.
estimated damaged vertex = healthy vertex + predicted movement

The known damaged mesh supplies training targets and accuracy checks; it is not an inspection input in the normal prediction condition. Camera information is embodied in the supplied rendering and healthy-surface correspondences; the current regression does not separately ingest a camera tensor. This is reference-conditioned deformation estimation, not reconstruction from an arbitrary photograph.

Seven views is the current setup, not a universal minimum. A zero predicted movement can mean “unchanged” or “not enough evidence”; the current output does not automatically distinguish them.

Existing geometry output

An estimated deformed mesh

The predictor already returns 3D vertex movements. The saved viewer shows the healthy mesh, known synthetic change and predicted shape.

Reliability to establish

Supported physical changes

Keep that mesh output, check which movements are accurate, and identify unresolved estimates. Supporting image overlays explain the geometry result.

The research test

Fewer invented changes

Can we reduce false 3D damage caused by camera, material or lighting mismatch while still finding actual damage?

My recommendation: keep the current CAD + paired views → deformed mesh contract. Strengthen the inference and evaluation of those vertex movements. Use image overlays to explain the result. First test one narrow mechanism on linked synthetic data; a real physical claim needs a new, independently measured capture packet.

05 / A focused candidate contribution

Recover physical changes, not just a good-looking fit.

A particularly close recent paper: CADSplat, section 6, explicitly leaves measurement-quality deformed surfaces for future work. That is a concrete limitation to investigate. It does not prove an untouched field: our opportunity is accurate small changes, preservation of healthy surface, and justified uncertainty under the same input budget.

A dark region can come from changed surface appearance, geometry, illumination, or an alignment error. A plausible reconstruction may choose the wrong explanation. Our own saved dent examples show why this matters: the model can improve the dent while moving healthy parts of the blade.

Can a shared model of local blade changes recover the physical damage supported by the views, while leaving ambiguous changes unresolved—and transfer that ability to another blade shape?

This strengthens the current vertex-movement experiment and the earlier local-change proposal. The current CAD + seven paired views → deformed mesh contract stays fixed. Geometry/material factorization is a possible implementation tool. The new hypothesis to test is whether explicitly accounting for competing explanations improves physical-change decisions beyond ordinary inverse rendering and simple uncertainty controls.

Explanation A

The shape need not have changed

Allow plausible camera, light and surface-appearance changes while keeping nominal geometry fixed.

This does not mean “healthy.” A visible appearance defect may remain real even when its physical depth cannot be inferred.

Explanation B

A local shape change is necessary

Allow the same camera, light and appearance changes, plus a localized geometric change.

Require support across observations and compare against held-out truth. Extra flexibility alone will always tend to improve a fit.

Novelty status: not yet established. Hypothesis testing, inverse rendering, separate appearance/shape variables and uncertainty are all established tools. A contribution would require a specific effective inference mechanism and a measured advantage. Merely combining those tools, changing the application to blades, or adding an “unknown” color does not establish it.
Technical candidate: estimate the range of physical changes the evidence can distinguish

Let d describe local deformation or removal, and η describe camera, illumination, material and allowed reference mismatch. Compare a fixed-shape explanation against a shape-change explanation under the same nuisance freedoms.

fit gain = minη L(d = 0, η) − mind, η L(d, η)

This score alone is not a decision rule. Calibrate its complexity/noise effects and evaluate it on independent objects. Around a fitted state, compare image sensitivity to damage with sensitivity to nuisance changes: if they produce indistinguishable image effects, that physical parameter is unresolved.

Evaluate synthetic Δxyz against matched-vertex truth, but also measure surface distance and normal displacement. Tangential vertex movement need not change surface shape and may be unidentifiable from images; a mesh correspondence is not automatically a measured material-point trajectory.

A candidate implementation would condition a shared local-change predictor on the healthy surface, propagate uncertainty in camera/material/reference fit, and output supported change ranges with a visibility map. Missing material requires a representation that can create new boundaries; a fixed mesh displacement alone is insufficient.

Hard risks: overly flexible appearance can explain away true damage; overly rigid appearance invents shape. Manufacturing variation can resemble a defect. Weakly observed regions must not become automatically “healthy.” These assumptions must be fixed and challenged rather than tuned until a desired conclusion appears.

Compare with ordinary fitting plus a pose-perturbation ensemble and observation-coverage rejection. Bayes’ Rays is a relevant uncertainty precedent; any adaptation to our damage representation must be labelled as an adaptation. Our theory must explain what extra ambiguity is handled.

Three examples of the intended behavior

Visible scratch or corrosion-like patch

Return its supported footprint and the evidence images. If shape change is unresolved, report that explicitly. Do not infer chemistry from color alone.

A dent seen from informative angles

Return a localized shape-change estimate and its uncertainty. Report physical depth only after scale, capture and independent checks justify it.

A chip near a partially hidden edge

Distinguish where material is absent from the exposed surface that remains. Leave unseen interior/volume unresolved when the views do not constrain it.

These describe proposed behavior. They are not new model predictions, clinical-style diagnoses, or established damage measurements.

07 / Advance the idea without drifting

One small experiment before a bigger model.

Start with the existing linked dent/healthy geometry to test physical ambiguity. Add appearance-only controls and controlled capture changes. Keep scratches, corrosion and removal in the overall scope; passing a dent pilot does not establish those other families.

Freeze the question and its evaluation

Primary question: fewer false physical-change claims at matched true-damage sensitivity and accepted coverage. “Accepted coverage” is the proportion of eligible regions for which the system gives a physical estimate. Lock case roles, noise/reference assumptions, budgets, thresholds and a meaningful improvement margin before running the untouched evaluation.

Construct difficult paired examples

Actual dent versus similar appearance-only patch; intact geometry with small alignment errors; true damage that flexible appearance could hide; weak-baseline versus informative views; nuisance shifts outside the training range. Verify exact geometry and visibility. These controls are planned, not already a complete dataset.

Compare against strong, equal-input methods

Use unchanged CAD/current displacement as diagnostics; a multiview image detector projected to the reference; direct geometry/appearance fitting; fitting with a simple pose ensemble and coverage rejection; and the candidate with its proposed ambiguity mechanism removed. All research comparisons get the same images, CAD, healthy reference, labels and tuning allowance. Report training and per-blade fitting cost.

Measure the right outcomes

Report false changed area on healthy surface, damage sensitivity, surface localization error, geometric error on supported regions, estimate coverage and uncertainty calibration. Include unseen regions, missed defects and failures. Check withheld views as a consistency diagnostic; use independently known surfaces for physical accuracy.

Make a real go / stop decision

Continue only if the advantage survives the ablation and remains at matched sensitivity/coverage. If a simple fit or pose ensemble performs equally well, the candidate has not earned a new-method claim. Stop architecture expansion, retain the useful engineering baseline and report the negative result.

Independence matters

Separate whole damage instances and source CAD families, not neighboring views of the same object. Existing exposed blade cases stay development data. Select fresh evaluation sizes using development variability; report object-level uncertainty rather than treating thousands of pixels as independent blades.

The real validation gate

After a positive synthetic mechanism test, obtain one same-blade capture with reference identity, calibrated views, scale and independently reserved damage measurements. Then repeat on independent blades and acquisition sessions. If this data cannot be obtained, explicitly choose an RGB-localization or synthetic-method scope.

Fair comparisons when the inspection inputs differ
  • Primary track: calibrated RGB + nominal geometry + healthy appearance reference, with equivalent information for every comparator.
  • RGB-only reference: shows the deployment tradeoff, but cannot isolate the proposed mechanism when the candidate receives more information.
  • Measured-depth methods: M3DM, SiM3D, ModMap and PASDF belong in a separate track if true point clouds/depth are supplied. Giving them estimated depth changes their setting; label it as an adaptation.
  • If the narrower proposal is retained: compare identical synthetic images and real-label budgets with 2D loss, generic geometry teaching, true damage-change supervision, shuffled links and an equal-capacity branch without geometric supervision. Do not claim a real 3D output from mask accuracy.
  • Meaningful thresholds: choose tolerances from the inspection task and capture resolution before fresh evaluation. This survey has not preregistered numerical thresholds or justified a sample size.

08 / Explain it to a supervisor

The project in four sentences.

We want to inspect blade photographs and identify what changed on the blade. Our current synthetic model takes a healthy CAD and seven paired views and predicts an estimated damaged 3D mesh. Our research question is whether we can avoid inventing physical damage when the camera or appearance changes, while still detecting real damage. We have synthetic and real-image components; the new mechanism and reliable real 3D measurements still need to be demonstrated.

Decision: keep the current 3D input/output contract and test whether its physical-change estimates can become reliable. Keep the existing RGB mask model as a practical baseline. Treat a real 3D result as a separate, measurable milestone.
